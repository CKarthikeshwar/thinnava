Thinnava on the web: laptop

The same four pages as the Android app (Home, Search, Map, Profile) and the same colours, fonts and words, laid out the way people expect a website to work. Nothing new to learn: if you know the app, you know the website.

Top bar, not a bottom barThe four pages sit as links in a top bar. Logging and pasting are always in the top right, on every page.
Use the widthThe phone's single column becomes a main column plus a side column, a filter sidebar, or a list beside the map.
Mouse and keyboardActions show on hover. / searches, N logs a visit, and Ctrl+V anywhere reads a pasted message.
Same app underneathSame data, same privacy rules, same forms (they open as a centred dialog instead of a bottom sheet).

Why one button for everyone

Newcomers and existing Android users do the same thing: Continue with Google. A new account goes on to "Set up your profile" (same as the app); an existing one lands straight on Home with all their places. "Sign in" at the top is the same button, for people who look there out of habit.

What's not here, on purpose

No feature tour, no screenshots carousel, no sign-up form. One promise, one example, one button.
